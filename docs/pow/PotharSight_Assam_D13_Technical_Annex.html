Common grid and pre-processing

  • Each district boundary (geoBoundaries ADM2, LGD-based) plus a 1000 m buffer defines a 30 m grid in UTM 46N.
  • Categorical layers (WorldCover, Esri LULC) are resampled with nearest neighbour; continuous layers (NDVI, DEM, soil, rain) bilinearly.
  • Sentinel-2 NDVI: per season (kharif Jun–Oct, rabi Nov–Mar) for 2022, 2023, 2024, clouds < 40 %, up to 3 scenes per tile per season; the seasonal peak is kept.
  • Sentinel-1 flood frequency: share of monsoon (6, 7, 8, 9) VV scenes below the water threshold, 2022, 2023, 2024.
  • Soil: SoilGrids 2.0 mean of 0–5, 5–15 and 15–30 cm; rainfall: CHIRPS annual mean 2014–2023.
  • Distances (water, road, town) are Euclidean distance transforms on the grid from OSM and JRC layers.
B

B. Land availability rules

Every 30 m cell gets one status, in this order (a later rule overrides an earlier one):

StatusRule (values from config.yaml → availability)
Excluded (0)WorldCover tree (10), built-up (50), water (80), wetland (90), mangrove (95); protected areas (OSM); JRC water occurrence ≥ 40 %; Esri built-up in any of the last 3 years; Esri trees or water in all of the last 3 years; Esri water in ≥ 1 of the 7 yearly maps (shifting river channels)
Vacant open land (1)WorldCover classes [20, 30, 100] (shrub, grass, moss), or Esri rangeland [11] in the latest year, and not cropland. Bare ground is left out: in Assam floodplains it is mostly river sand.
Long fallow / under-used (2)Cropland whose kharif peak NDVI < 0.5, or land that was cropland (WorldCover 40 or Esri crops in any year) and is Esri rangeland [11] in the latest year
Rabi fallow (3)Cropland not in (2) whose rabi peak NDVI < 0.35 – kharif paddy left empty in winter
Active (4)All other cropland – not a candidate; used only to test that the criteria reward productive land

Why 0.50 for long fallow: the 5th percentile of kharif peak NDVI on active farmland in Kamrup is 0.52, so cropland below 0.50 is unlikely to have carried a kharif crop.

Flood data-quality gate

gate passes ⇔ median( S1 flood frequency on WorldCover cropland ) ≤ 0.3

Ordinary cropland should not look flooded in most monsoon radar scenes. Where waterlogged paddy makes radar read as flood, the gate fails and the flood layer falls back to JRC occurrence plus Esri water history. The result is stored per run in flood_qa.json.

C. Factor scoring

Each indicator becomes a 0–1 score with one of two functions (d13/core.py), then indicators combine into six factors.

ramp(x; lo, hi) = clip( (x − lo) / (hi − lo), 0, 1 ) (lo > hi gives a decreasing ramp)
trapezoid(x; [L0, L1], [O0, O1]) = min( clip((x − L0)/(O0 − L0), 0, 1), clip((L1 − x)/(L1 − O1), 0, 1) )
IndicatorFunctionParameters (config.yaml → scoring)Feeds
Soil pHtrapezoid0 at 4.5 and 8.5; 1 between 6.0 and 7.5soil × 0.35
Soil organic carbon (g/kg)ramp0 at 5 → 1 at 15soil × 0.25
Nitrogen (g/kg)ramp0 at 0.5 → 1 at 2.5soil × 0.15
CEC (cmol/kg)ramp0 at 5 → 1 at 25soil × 0.15
Clay (%)trapezoid0 at 5 and 60; 1 between 15 and 35soil × 0.1
Annual rainfall (mm)trapezoid0 at 1000 and 4000; 1 between 1500 and 2500water × 0.4
Distance to water (m)ramp (decreasing)1 within 250 → 0 beyond 3000water × 0.6
Monsoon flood frequencyramp (decreasing)1 at 0.0 → 0 at 0.4flood × 1
Slope (%)ramp (decreasing)1 at 2 → 0 at 15terrain × 1
Peak NDVI (max of kharif, rabi)ramp0 at 0.3 → 1 at 0.85productivity × 1
Distance to road (m)ramp (decreasing)1 within 250 → 0 beyond 5000access × 0.6
Distance to town (m)ramp (decreasing)1 within 3000 → 0 beyond 25000access × 0.4
soil = Σ w_i · s_i / Σ w_i with w = pH 0.35, SOC 0.25, N 0.15, CEC 0.15, clay 0.1
water = 0.4 · rain + 0.6 · distance-to-water access = 0.6 · road + 0.4 · town
flood, terrain, productivity = their single indicator

Soil norms follow Soil Health Card / ICAR ratings (organic carbon 0.5 % = low, > 0.75 % = high; acidity is the main soil constraint in Assam). Rainfall uses a trapezoid so both deficit and excess are penalised. Gaps in coarse layers (soil, rain edges) are filled with the district median so one missing value never removes a cell.

D. Suitability, prioritisation and grades

Weighted score and class

score = 100 · Σ_f w_f · factor_f / Σ_f w_f
class = S1 if score ≥ 80 · S2 if ≥ 65 · S3 if ≥ 50 · otherwise N
if min(factor) < 40: class ≤ S2 if min(factor) < 20: class ≤ S3 (FAO most-limiting factor)
constrained (class 5) if slope > max slope or flood frequency > max flood
ProfileMax slope %Max flood freq.Soil fertilityWater availabilityFlood safetyGentle terrainVegetation productivityRoad / market access
Field crops150.80.30.20.150.150.10.1
Horticulture300.30.30.150.20.10.10.15

Reasons

limiting factor = argmax_f (1 − factor_f) · w_f contributing factor = argmax_f factor_f · w_f

Sites (patches) and their priority

patch = 8-connected group of candidate cells (status 1–3) in class S1/S2, area ≥ 0.5 ha
patch class = S1 if ≥ 50 % of its cells are S1, else S2; patch status = most common status
patch priority = 0.8 · mean score + 20 · min(area_ha, 10) / 10

Blocks and revenue circles

priority index = (S1 ha + 0.5 · S2 ha) · mean score / 100 rank = descending priority index

Grades shown to officials

Grades split the S1/S2 sites near their quartiles so an official sees a spread of quality, not one colour. Factor levels on site cards: High ≥ 80, Medium 55–80, Low < 55. Watch-outs are shown for factors < 60; strong points for factors ≥ 80.

Limitations

  • “Available” means vacant or under-used in satellite data; ownership and legal status must be verified by the Revenue Department.
  • Char and riverine land changes every year; some sites on river islands will be wrong until re-run with fresh imagery.
  • Assamese text is a first translation for the prototype and should be reviewed by the department.
  • The prototype covers four districts; the same code runs any district by adding one entry in config.yaml.
